# IELTS Academic Writing Task 2 — Structure Guide

**Source:** *IELTS-Complete-Guide-Clean-Edition.pdf*, Writing Task 2 section, printed pages 37–46. These notes reorganize and summarize the guide's methods and five essay structures.

Use this as a planning aid, not a script to memorize. Follow the exact question, answer every part, and use your own ideas and language. A structure helps organize an answer; it does not guarantee a band score.

## Universal method for every Task 2 essay

### Before writing
1. Read the whole prompt and notice every instruction and question.
2. Identify the essay type and what the examiner asks you to do.
3. Plan two main ideas that directly answer the prompt. Choose relevant explanations and examples before drafting.

### Introduction
4. Paraphrase the question in your own words.
5. Give a direct answer or clear position. For opinion questions, state how far you agree or disagree. For multi-part questions, answer each part briefly.

### Main body
6. Begin each main paragraph with one clear main idea that supports the answer.
7. Explain the idea: why it matters, how it works, or what its effects are. Develop it before moving to a new point.
8. Give a specific example or evidence that supports the explanation.

Use this development pattern: **main idea → explanation → relevant example**. Avoid jumping from a claim straight to an example, using an unrelated example, or squeezing several undeveloped ideas into one paragraph.

### Conclusion
9. Summarize the main ideas and answer the question one final time. Do not introduce new information or copy the introduction word for word.

## The five essay structures in the guide

### 1. Advantages and disadvantages

**Plan:** Identify the main advantages and disadvantages. If the prompt asks whether one side outweighs the other, decide your answer before writing.

- **Introduction:** Paraphrase the topic and briefly preview the advantages and disadvantages. For an “outweigh” question, state your verdict clearly.
- **Body paragraph 1 — advantages:** Present the main advantage, explain it, then develop a second related advantage. Support the explanation with a relevant example.
- **Body paragraph 2 — disadvantages:** Present the main disadvantage, explain it, then develop a second related disadvantage. Support the explanation with a relevant example.
- **Conclusion:** Summarize both sides. If asked to judge which side outweighs the other, restate that judgment clearly.

**Check:** Have you developed both sides? Is your verdict explicit when the question asks you to compare their weight?

### 2. Discuss both views and give your opinion

**Plan:** Work out why people support each view, then decide which view you agree with and why.

- **Introduction:** Paraphrase the issue, briefly introduce both views, and state your own opinion.
- **Body paragraph 1 — view one:** Explain why people hold this view, develop the reason, give a relevant example, and make your position on this view clear.
- **Body paragraph 2 — view two:** Explain the other view, develop the reason, give a relevant example, and show how it relates to your opinion.
- **Conclusion:** Summarize both views and restate your opinion.

**Check:** Are both views explained fairly? Is your opinion clear in the introduction, body, and conclusion?

### 3. Two-part / double-question essay

**Plan:** Write a direct answer to each question before choosing supporting details.

- **Introduction:** Paraphrase the topic and briefly answer Question 1 and Question 2.
- **Body paragraph 1 — Question 1:** State the answer to Question 1, explain it, develop the explanation, and give a relevant example.
- **Body paragraph 2 — Question 2:** State the answer to Question 2, explain it, develop the explanation, and give a relevant example.
- **Conclusion:** Summarize both answers.

**Check:** Have you answered both questions directly and fully? Does each body paragraph answer the question assigned to it?

### 4. Opinion essay

**Plan:** Decide your position and choose two distinct reasons that support it.

- **Introduction:** Paraphrase the question, state your clear opinion, and preview the two main reasons.
- **Body paragraph 1 — reason one:** State the reason, explain how or why it supports your opinion, develop the point, and give a relevant example.
- **Body paragraph 2 — reason two:** State a different reason, explain and develop it, and give a relevant example.
- **Conclusion:** Restate your opinion and summarize the two reasons.

**Check:** Is your position consistent from start to finish? Does each example support the reason in its paragraph?

### 5. Problem and solution essay

**Plan:** Identify the main problems and choose solutions that address them.

- **Introduction:** Paraphrase the issue and briefly preview the problems and solutions.
- **Body paragraph 1 — problems:** Explain the main problems and their effects. Develop the points and use a relevant example where it helps.
- **Body paragraph 2 — solutions:** Present solutions that address the problems, explain how they would work, and support them with a relevant example.
- **Conclusion:** Summarize the problems and solutions.

**Check:** Are the problems clearly explained? Does each proposed solution address a problem you identified?

## Mapping other prompt wording to these structures

The guide presents five structures above. For question wording not named as a separate structure there, choose the closest structure by what the prompt asks:

- **Positive or negative development:** use the Opinion structure; state a clear judgment and support it with two reasons.
- **Causes and effects:** use the Two-part structure when the prompt asks for causes and effects as separate questions.
- **Causes and solutions:** use the Problem and solution structure, explaining causes as part of the problem paragraph and matching solutions to them.
- **Cause, effect, or solution questions with different instructions:** answer the exact parts asked; adapt the closest structure instead of forcing a mismatched template.

These mappings are practical adaptations; the guide does not give them as separate blank structures.

## General self-check after drafting

- Did I identify the correct task and answer every part?
- Does the introduction paraphrase the prompt and state the required answer or opinion?
- Do my body paragraphs have clear topic sentences and developed ideas?
- Does each main idea have an explanation and relevant support or example?
- Are the ideas logically ordered and connected without mechanical linking phrases?
- Does the conclusion match the position and main ideas in the essay, without adding a new idea?
- Have I checked grammar, vocabulary, spelling, and meaning?

## Daily practice and progress

For each practice essay, record the question type, one structural strength, and the most important structural issue to fix. On a rewrite, compare the first and revised versions: note which structural change was made and whether it improved the answer. Practise one question type with several prompts before moving to another.

The guide emphasizes that planning, clear structure, and developed paragraphs help organize an essay, but do not replace language accuracy or guarantee a particular score.
